What you can mark

  • Mushrooms — chanterelle, funnel chanterelle, porcini, black and red trumpet, hedgehog mushroom, parasol, and a dozen more.
  • Berries — blueberry, lingonberry, cloudberry, raspberry, blackberry, wild strawberry, highbush blueberry.
  • Fish — perch, pike, salmon, brown trout, rainbow trout, arctic char.
  • Places to stop — rest areas, campsites, motorhome stops and wild camping spots.
  • Anything else — add your own kinds with a name, a symbol and a colour. It does not have to be a species: a swimming spot, a woodshed or a gate works just as well.

Common questions

Can the app tell me which mushroom I found?

No. NaturaPin is a map for marking places on, not an app that identifies species. The kind you pick is your own note about what you thought you found, and the app never looks at the photo you take. What is safe to eat is yours to decide — with a field guide, with someone who knows, or at a mushroom inspection. Several of the good mushrooms have poisonous lookalikes, and some of those are deadly.

Where are my finds stored?

On your own device only. There is no server and no cloud sync, and nobody but you can reach them. If you change phones or reinstall the app you have to move them yourself with a backup.

How do I make a backup?

Under the three dots in My finds you will find Back up. The file holds everything: finds, photos, weather, time, position and direction. Put it in iCloud Drive or somewhere else you can reach without the phone. Always make one before you switch phones.

Why did my find get no weather?

Weather is fetched over the network, and out in the forest there is rarely a signal. The find is saved anyway, and the weather is filled in the next time you open the app with a connection — but only if the find is less than an hour and a half old, because the forecast data does not reach backwards in time. Pins you place by hand never get weather, because the weather where you are standing says nothing about a place you are not.

How accurate is the position?

When you mark a find the app waits for three readings that are each accurate to within ten metres and saves the median of them. If that has not happened within eight seconds it uses the best reading it managed to get. However accurate it turned out to be is always written out in the detail view, in metres.

Does it work on iPad and Mac?

On iPad, yes, with the same camera and GPS as the phone. Held wide enough it shows the list, the map and the details side by side. A Mac version exists but is not sold on the App Store today.

Which languages does it speak?

The app is written in Swedish. Fifteen more languages are included, translated with the help of AI without a native speaker having read them through. If you spot something that came out wrong, please write and tell me.

Privacy

Your finds never leave the device. The only thing sent out is the coordinate of a find to the weather service, at the moment the weather is fetched — and that can be turned off. See the privacy policy.
